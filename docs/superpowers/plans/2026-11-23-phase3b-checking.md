# Phase 3B: Checking the marking — Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** A sample of each exam is marked again by a second marker who sees nothing of the first; markers practise before live marking and meet seeded scripts they cannot tell from the rest; the lead marker settles differences, reviews and sends work back; admins see marker statistics, approve an exam's marking once every script is `checked`, set pay rates and download monthly statements.

**Architecture:** A second, seed or practice pass is one more `markings` row of that kind with its own `marking_assignments` row; a marker has at most one pass on a piece, so `/marking/{unitId}` and every marker-facing answer look the same whatever the kind. Each piece gets a check state beside its live state; when it becomes `checked` its final mark per question is written to `final_marks`, which is the only thing statistics totals, approval and (later) results read. Markers' own marks are never altered by anyone else: the first mark, the second mark, the lead's decision and the final mark are four separate records. Marking pictures are signed by an Edge Function after a database permission check; the markers' storage policy goes.

**Tech Stack:** Supabase (Postgres 17, pg_cron, Storage, Edge Functions on Deno), pgTAP; Next.js 16, React 19, Vitest, Playwright. No new npm, Deno or Python packages. The processing server is not touched.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-20-web-platform-design.md`, section 6.2 ("Small subjects", "Practice first"), 6.5 and 6.6 (approved 2026-11-20); also binding: 3 (anti-goals), the last paragraph of 11, 12 to 15, and open decisions 17.4 and 17.8. Master plan: `docs/owner/2026-11-functional-specification.pdf`, sections 17 to 21 (the design wins where it already decided). Phase 3A hand-over: `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-21-phase3a-marking.md` ("Where Phase 3B plugs in"), `docs/superpowers/2026-11-22-phase3a-release-notes.md` ("Carry into Phase 3B", "Known limits"), `docs/superpowers/2026-11-22-phase3a-rulings.md` (R9, R10, R12, R14 to R17, R23, R25). Builds on branch `phase3-marking` (not merged or deployed).

**Not in this plan:** results, grade boundaries, rank, publishing, family re-marks (Phase 4; the `remark` kind stays unused); a comments library, offline marking, AI suggestions (Phase 5); online payment of markers (paying happens outside the system). **If the plan must be split,** Tasks 11 and 12 (payments) stand alone: nothing before them reads a payments table, and Task 13 only lists them in the release notes.

## Builder's calls in this plan

Each is a choice the design left open. The owner can override any of them.

| # | Call | If wrong, it costs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The second marker is any other approved marker of the subject, the lead included,** who is cleared and not paused on the exam; never the first marker, never someone with a declared conflict or tutor link for the candidate, never someone who saw a name on the script. A marker cannot tell a second marking from a first: the screen and the lists are the same. | Showing "Second marking" on the piece is one field. |
| 2 | **The sample is drawn per piece** (a whole script, or one question group of a script), not per script. Each piece gets a stored random number when it is made; it is in the sample when that number is under the exam's percentage **at the moment its first marking is submitted**, and the decision is then kept. The lead or an admin can add any piece by hand ("Second-mark these") or drop a waiting one with a reason. | A small exam may get no second marking by chance (10% of 8 is often none); the approval page shows how many were second-marked. Sampling per script is one join. |
| 3 | **"Outside tolerance"** uses the exam's percentage with a floor of one mark: the two totals of the piece differ by more than `greatest(1, percentage × the piece's maximum)`, or any question differs by more than `greatest(1, percentage × that question's maximum)`. NR counts as 0 for the sum; NR against 0 agrees; NR against any mark above 0 always goes to the lead. Half marks are compared exactly, with no rounding. | Without the floor every 1-mark difference on a short question would go to the lead. The floor is one constant. |
| 4 | **Within tolerance the first marks stand.** Nothing is averaged. | Averaging is one branch where final marks are written. |
| 5 | **A practice or seeded script is a copy of the lead's own submitted live marking of a piece.** The lead marks the piece as any marker does, then presses "Use for practice" or "Use as a seeded script"; the marks are copied and frozen. The candidate's live marking and final marks are never touched by a practice or seed pass. Such a piece is never in the second-marking sample. At most 10 of each kind per exam (and per question group). | The lead's marking of that piece is not itself second-marked. Letting an admin pick another marker's piece is one condition. |
| 6 | **Seeds come through "Next script" only,** one after about every 10 pieces (set per exam, 3 to 50; the gap is redrawn at random between half and one and a half times that), each seed once per marker. Inside tolerance the marker is told nothing. Outside, they are shown the lead's marks beside their own and that attempt is locked. | A marker who only works on assigned pieces meets no seeds; mixing seeds into "Assign the next n" is one more branch. |
| 7 | **A miss is a seed outside the tolerance of call 3. Three misses in a row on one exam pause the marker on that exam** (not on their other exams). The pause stops their work at once; ending their holds and sending their submitted pieces to the lead happens within a minute (the minute job), so no function ever locks two scripts out of order. | Up to a minute before the lead's list fills. A pause across all exams of the subject is one condition. |
| 8 | **When a marker is paused,** every piece of that exam they submitted as first marker and the lead has not reviewed goes to the lead, also pieces already `checked`. Their submitted second markings are set aside and those pieces wait for another second marker. The lead accepts each first marking or sends it to another marker. Clearing the marker lets them work again; the held pieces stay with the lead. | More review work for the lead after a pause. Limiting it to pieces since the last good seed is one condition. |
| 9 | **Practice is announced** (the piece says "Practice"). A marker does every practice script of the exam, sees the differences after each, and then waits for the lead, who clears them by hand with a reason, whatever the marks. The lead needs no practice. The lead or an admin can also clear a marker without practice, with a reason. | A lead who is slow to clear holds markers up; clearing automatically inside tolerance is one branch. |
| 10 | **The small-subject switches** (double marking, seeds, practice) can be turned off only while the subject has at most one approved marker, as the design says, and need a reason. Opening marking with double marking on needs two approved markers; with seeds or practice on it needs a lead. | A two-marker subject where one falls ill must unapprove them first. Loosening it is one condition. |
| 11 | **An admin may do everything the lead does in review** (the lead may be away, or have a conflict for that candidate). **A lead who is one of the two markers may settle the difference;** the approval page counts those. | A stricter rule (an admin must settle the lead's own differences) is one check. |
| 12 | **Send back has two forms:** "to the same marker" (their marking opens again with the lead's note; the default in routine review) and "to another marker" (their marking is set aside and they are not offered the piece again; the only form for a paused marker). A second marking already made is kept and compared again. | One more choice on the send-back box. |
| 13 | **Final marks have their own table.** Nobody but the marker changes a marker's own marks. When the lead changes a mark it is the final mark that changes, with who, when and why, on a piece in any checked state, until the exam is approved. | None expected (the master plan asks for the four records kept apart). |
| 14 | **A piece is `checked`** when its first marking is submitted and nothing waits: not in the sample, or second-marked within tolerance, or settled or accepted by the lead. A script is `checked` when every piece is. A piece stops being checked when its first marking is taken back, sent back or reopened, or its marker changes a mark (it is checked again at once, and compared again if it was second-marked). Once the lead has settled a piece, its markers can no longer change their marks on it. | A marker who spots their own slip after a settle must ask the lead. |
| 15 | **Approving closes marking and freezes everything** (marks, final marks, questions, check settings) until an admin withdraws the approval with a reason. Withdrawn and void scripts do not block and are counted on the page. An open routine sample review does not block. A script that turns up after approval is flagged on the page, never added silently. | One more step (withdraw, reopen) to fix something late. |
| 16 | **Statistics are per piece, never per script:** a marker's average is compared with the average of all markers on the same question groups. Three neutral notes ("Worth a look") appear only from 10 pieces: much faster than the exam's usual, many full marks, average far from the cohort. They are shown to the lead and admins only and change nothing by themselves. | The thresholds are three constants. |
| 17 | **Pay:** one rate per script (or per question group) for first and second marking alike. A marking is paid when it is submitted and still stands. Practice and seeds are never paid. A marking set aside (taken back, sent to another marker, name seen) is not paid; a piece sent back to the same marker is paid once. The month is the UK calendar month of submission. The lead's review work is not paid by the system. | A marker loses pay for a piece taken away through no fault of theirs; an admin settles that outside the system. A second-marking rate is one column. |
| 18 | **A month's statements are fixed by an admin** after the month ends. Fixed statements never change; a piece missed by a fixed month is carried into the next. Statements and the CSV are for admins; markers have no payments page. | A page for markers to read their own statements is one function and one list. |

## Where Phase 4 plugs in

- Results read `public.script_final_marks(script)` and `public.script_final_total(script)` and nothing else. They never add piece totals (`markings.total`): a piece's total applies "answer 2 of these" inside the piece only.
- Results may be prepared only while `exam_marking.approved_at` is set; `marking_approval_withdraw` must be refused by Phase 4 once results are published.
- The annotated script a family sees is the layer of `marking_units.final_marking_id`.
- A family re-mark is a `remark` marking; `marking_units_allowed` and the payable rule already list the kind.

## Global Constraints

- From the design, word for word: "Server time decides every deadline (UK time on screen, daylight saving handled)." "Private buckets only; pictures through signed links that expire within minutes, handed out after a permission check." "Markers receive marking pages only (name band removed) and see index numbers, never names." "Every staff action that changes a script, a mark, a structure, a boundary or a publication writes an audit row with a reason where the action asks for one." "No personal data in server logs." From 6.5: "a second marker who sees neither the first marks nor the first layer"; "mixed into markers' queues unannounced"; "The lead marker's routine sample of each marker's work does not hold scripts back; a change the lead makes is recorded on the script whatever its state."; "All changes are recorded with reasons."; "an admin approves an exam's marking when every script is `checked`. Only then can results be prepared." From 6.6: "practice and seeds excluded"; "Paying markers happens outside the system."
- Anti-goals (a failure even if it works): "A marker sees a candidate's name, or can tell whose script it is from anything on screen except the index number." "The original upload or scan is changed or lost; a mark changes without a record of who, when and why." "A script is marked twice by mistake, or not at all without anyone noticing." "Results reach families before an admin approved them."
- Master plan, section 21: "Keep first mark, second mark, moderation decision and final approved mark separately." "No automated statistical flag should itself accuse an examiner or candidate of misconduct." "Require human review for consequential changes."
- Row-level security on every new table. Writes only through `security definer` SQL functions with `set search_path = ''`; `revoke execute ... from public, anon`, then explicit grants; internal helpers are revoked from `authenticated` and `service_role` too. Markers get no table privileges that return rows: every new table's select policy is `(select public.is_staff_admin())`.
- An `audit_log` row with `reason` in the same transaction as every staff, lead or admin change. Reasons: trimmed, 3 to 300 characters (`public.script_resolution_reason`). Notes to a marker and notes on a standard: 3 to 1,000 characters.
- Nothing marker- or lead-facing (function result, page, email, log, CSV) carries a candidate's first name, last name, email, booking id or student id. The second marker never receives the first marker's marks, total, annotations or note. A seeded piece carries no key, value, word, order or timing that a live piece does not: `marking_unit_open`, `marking_my_work`, `marking_next`, `marking_save`, `marking_submit` and the picture function answer with the same keys for every kind. Audit reasons and `marker_conflicts.note` may hold a name: they never appear in a marker or lead view.
- The web app never holds the service-role key. It is used only inside Edge Functions.
- Lock order (R9, R10), extended: marker advisory key (`hashtextextended('marker:' || id, 0)`) → `exam_marking` → booking advisory lock → script rows in id order → units in id order → assignments and markings → `marking_reviews` → `final_marks` → `exam_markers`. A function that already holds one script's row never takes another script's: work across scripts starts from `marking_tick` or from an admin or lead action that begins at the top.
- Everything that tests a script for `'ready_to_mark', 'marking', 'marked'` must also accept `'checked'` (Task 4 lists them). Name-seen rules (R14, R15) and the picture check (R16, R17) apply to every kind of pass.
- Time rules take `p_now timestamptz default null` read through `public.paper_clock`. Times are `timestamptz`, shown in `Europe/London` (`apps/web/src/lib/london-time.ts`). Money is whole pence in `int`, shown as pounds.
- Exact values: sample 10% by default (1 to 100); tolerance 10% by default (1 to 50), floor 1 mark; three misses in a row; a seed about every 10 pieces (3 to 50); at most 10 practice and 10 seeded scripts per exam and group; hold 2 hours; picture links 120 seconds; at most 40 pages per link request; a routine sample of 5 pieces (1 to 20); a rate from 0 to 100,000 pence; statistics notes from 10 pieces; lists capped at 500 with a `more` flag; the screen's access check is due at 55 seconds on a 15-second tick.

- Tests run on the LOCAL stack only: `npm run db:test:local`, `npm run fn:test:local`, `npm test -w @stem/web`, `npm test -w @stem/shared`. `apps/web/.env.local` points at the LIVE project: never read it, never start `next dev` with it. ## Review Focus

1. A marker tries to spot a seed: compares two pieces on screen, in the browser's network panel, or by pressing "Give back" and "Next script" again. Nothing differs until they submit one outside tolerance. Tests: Task 5 `seed_piece_opens_with_the_same_keys_as_a_live_piece`, `given_back_seed_returns_later_and_tells_nothing`; Task 6 browser test `seed_looks_like_any_script_until_a_miss`.
2. A piece waits for a second marker and nobody can take it (the only other marker has a conflict for that candidate, was paused, or left): it is counted as stuck where the lead and admins look, and can be released with a reason; it never waits unseen. Tests: Task 4 `second_drop_checks_the_piece_with_a_reason`; Task 13 `waiting_piece_nobody_can_second_is_counted_as_stuck`.
3. A marker changes a mark while the lead is looking at the difference, or the lead and an admin settle the same piece at once: the later action is refused in plain words and no final mark is built on marks nobody saw. Tests: Task 7 `settle_refused_when_a_marking_changed_since_it_was_shown`, `settle_twice_is_refused`; Task 4 `marker_change_refused_once_the_lead_settled`.
4. A script is verified, handed back or un-withdrawn after the exam's marking was approved: the approval page says so and Phase 4 can read it; the script is not counted as approved. Test: Task 10 `script_arriving_after_approval_is_flagged`.
5. A marking counted in a fixed statement is later set aside, its rate is changed, or it was submitted in the last hour of a month across the clock change: the fixed statement does not move, no marking is ever paid twice, and the month is the UK one. Tests: Task 11 `fixed_statement_does_not_change`, `no_marking_is_ever_in_two_statements`, `month_is_the_uk_calendar_month`.
